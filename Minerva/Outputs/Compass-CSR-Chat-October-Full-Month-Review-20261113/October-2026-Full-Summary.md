# Compass CSR Chat: October 2026 month in review

Reporting period: October 1–31, 2026, Pacific time. Historical comparison: April 7–October 31, 2026. Prepared November 13, 2026.

## Executive assessment

October produced the highest observed monthly reach and query volume: **1,672 monthly active users**, up **25.2%**, and **37,741 queries**, up **17.5%** from September. Cumulative observed usage reached **2,460 unique users** and **154,515 queries** since April 7.

Acquisition drove the increase in reach. October added **534 newly observed users** and **76 reacquired users**, offset by **273 September users with no October activity**, yielding **337 net additional active users**. Monthly activity retention decreased from **82.2% to 79.6%**, a decline of **2.6 percentage points**.

Engagement has two different signals. Across all active users, mean queries per user fell from **24.1 to 22.6** and median active days fell from **6 to 4**. However, the same **1,062 users active in both September and October increased their combined query volume by 10.2%**. The larger new-user cohort and its shorter observation windows partly explain why overall depth can decline while established-user usage grows. These observations do not establish a causal explanation.

The strongest operational priority suggested by the data is converting first use and reacquisition into sustained use. New users' repeat usage within a common five-business-day window remained near September's level. Reacquired users remained a lighter-use cohort, and the frequent-use population grew much more slowly than total reach.

### Business-hours treatment

September and October each contain **22 weekdays**, so weekday averages have the same denominator. October had **558 Saturday queries**, or **1.5%** of its total, and **zero Sunday queries**. September had 377 Saturday queries and 18 Sunday queries. Monthly user counts and total queries retain those observed records.

Saturday hours were not supplied, so the primary analysis does not assign a factual Saturday hour weight. As a sensitivity check only, dividing all monthly queries by weekdays plus 0.25 or 0.5 day per Saturday yields growth of **16.3% or 15.1%**, respectively. The main conclusion of higher October usage is unchanged. These illustrative assumptions are not estimates of actual staffing hours. No holiday calendar or user work schedules were supplied, so no additional dates were removed. September 7's low activity, for example, remains in the weekday series.

## 2. Retention, lapse, acquisition and reacquisition

| Reporting month | Retained from prior month | Newly observed | Reacquired | Prior-month users now inactive | Retention |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| May | 341 | 219 | 0 | 63 | 84.4% |
| Jun | 414 | 296 | 14 | 146 | 73.9% |
| Jul | 621 | 447 | 38 | 103 | 85.8% |
| Aug | 919 | 333 | 38 | 187 | 83.1% |
| Sep | 1,060 | 227 | 48 | 230 | 82.2% |
| Oct | **1,062** | **534** | **76** | **273** | **79.6%** |

October active-user reconciliation: **1,062 retained + 534 newly observed + 76 reacquired = 1,672**. Net change: **534 + 76 − 273 = +337**.

The 534 newly observed users were 31.9% of October MAU, versus 17.0% for September's new cohort. New-user count increased **135.2%**. Reacquisition increased from 48 to 76, or **58.3%**. Lapses increased from 230 to 273, or **18.7%**. The retained population was almost unchanged in size, increasing from 1,060 to 1,062, despite much higher October reach.

Definitions:

- **Retained:** active in both the immediately prior month and reporting month.
- **Newly observed:** first recorded query in the reporting month, with no activity in any earlier supplied monthly extract. This is not a verified signup or onboarding record.
- **Reacquired:** active before the immediately prior month, inactive throughout the prior month, and active again in the reporting month.
- **Lapsed / activity churn:** active in the prior month and no recorded query in the reporting month. This is not proof of account cancellation, dissatisfaction, or permanent departure.

### Where lapse risk is concentrated

| September usage | September users | Inactive in October | Lapse rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 query | 121 | 61 | **50.4%** |
| 2–4 queries | 222 | 77 | **34.7%** |
| 5–19 queries | 462 | 100 | **21.6%** |
| 20+ queries | 530 | 35 | **6.6%** |

Light September users were much more likely to be inactive in October. Users with only 1–4 September queries account for **138 of the 273 lapses (50.5%)**, but only **269 September queries**. The **35 lapsed users who previously asked at least 20 questions** account for **1,331 September queries**, making them a smaller group with more prior usage worth investigating. Prior usage and return are associated; the data does not show that increasing query count by itself causes retention.

The full lapsed cohort generated **2,547 September queries**, or 7.9% of September volume. Thus 20.4% user churn did not imply losing 20.4% of query volume.

The full-month repeat measures have unequal observation time because users return on different dates. A common-window check includes only users with **five complete subsequent weekdays** available before month end. **26 of 64 eligible October reacquired users returned during those five weekdays (40.6%)**, versus **18 of 41 in September (43.9%)**. Twelve October users and seven September users did not have enough follow-up time and are excluded from that comparison. These small cohorts do not support a strong claim that reacquisition quality improved or worsened.

### Follow-through on September's 48 reacquired users

**28 of 48 (58.3%) were active in October**, while 20 had no October activity. The 28 active users generated **568 October queries**, with median **5 queries** and **3.5 active days per active user**. Those per-user medians exclude the 20 inactive users. For context, 163 of September's 227 newly observed users returned in October (71.8%), and 871 of September's 1,060 users retained from August returned again (82.2%). Reacquisition was therefore a less durable activity state than continued established usage in these observed cohorts.

Frequent use grew more slowly than reach. Users with five or more active days increased only **1.4%**, and users active in at least three calendar weeks increased **2.3%**, while MAU increased 25.2%. However, **219 of October's 534 new users (41.0%) first appeared October 21–31**, so full-month depth thresholds disadvantage many new arrivals. Three-calendar-week measures also include partial weeks and should be interpreted as a descriptive frequency measure rather than a matched opportunity comparison.

### The same retained users used Compass more

The 1,062 users active in both September and October generated **29,566 September queries** and **32,576 October queries**, up **10.2%**. Their median queries rose from 17.5 to 19. Of these users, 517 increased usage, 470 decreased, and 75 were unchanged. The increase is an aggregate result, not universal improvement among all retained users.

| Contribution to October's query change | Queries |
|---|---:|
| Increased usage by the same retained users | +3,010 |
| September queries from users inactive in October | −2,547 |
| October queries from newly observed users | +4,613 |
| October queries from reacquired users | +552 |
| **Total query increase** | **+5,628** |

Retained users supplied **86.3% of October queries**, newly observed users 12.2%, and reacquired users 1.5%. The new and reacquired groups represented 36.5% of users but only 13.7% of queries. This composition makes mean queries per MAU a poor standalone measure of established-user health.

### New-user activation with matched opportunity

October's new users had median **6 queries and 2 active days**. **342 of 534 (64.0%)** used Compass on another date before month end, similar to September's 64.3%. Using the same five-subsequent-weekday definition, **235 of 429 eligible October new users returned (54.8%)**, versus **99 of 186 in September (53.2%)**. The remaining 105 October new users had not yet completed that follow-up window by October 31. This comparison suggests broadly similar early repeat use despite the larger October acquisition cohort, rather than a demonstrated improvement in activation.

## 5. Response performance and available quality signals

Median logged answer time improved from **11 to 9 seconds (18.2% faster)**. P95 improved from **25 to 18 seconds (28.0% faster)**. Mean answer time improved from 12.49 to 10.43 seconds. The latency distribution improved while query volume increased.

There were **179 records with a blank answer in October (0.47%)**, compared with 102 in September (0.32%). Records showing a positive retry count increased from 26 (0.08%) to 71 (0.19%). These fields warrant investigation, but neither a blank answer nor a retry is a validated service-failure classification. The extract does not establish answer correctness or end-to-end reliability, so no reliability percentage is asserted.

October received **33 thumbs-up and 4 thumbs-down records**. That is 89.2% positive among only 37 submitted ratings, covering **0.10% of queries**. September had 61 positive and 2 negative ratings, covering 0.20% of queries. Selection bias and very sparse feedback prevent treating these percentages as representative satisfaction estimates or a reliable trend.

## 6. Proposed actions and measurable learning

The evidence supports prioritizing sustained use alongside continued acquisition. The following are proposals for product and operations review, not approved targets or causal conclusions.

| Proposal | Evidence and baseline | Proposed measure and decision trigger | Proposed owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Help new users complete a second useful session | 534 new users, but 54.8% of eligible users returned within five subsequent weekdays | Test an onboarding or workflow intervention against a comparison group. Aim for at least 60% five-weekday repeat use, using completed follow-up windows. Check lift and uncertainty before expanding. | Product and CSR operations |
| Test a follow-up workflow after reacquisition | Median 3 queries and 2 active days. Five-weekday repeat use is 40.6% among 64 eligible returnees. | Pilot follow-up around the returning user's observed workflow. Proposed goal: at least 50% repeat use in five weekdays, then measure next-month activity. Accumulate more than one month's evidence if the sample stays small. | Product and CSR operations |
| Investigate the smaller group of previously frequent users who lapsed | 35 lapsed users previously asked 20+ queries and generated 1,331 September queries | Check role changes, work assignment, access and workflow fit before deciding on outreach. Track next-month activity recovery and restored query volume. Broader campaign expansion requires evidence of useful recovery. | CSR operations, with product support |
| Protect response quality while reach expands | Median 9 seconds, P95 18 seconds. Blank-answer and retry indicators increased. | Review blank-answer cases and retries, and sample answer usefulness. Use October latency as a monitoring baseline while testing engagement changes. Review interventions if latency or usefulness worsens. | Engineering and product |

A broad acquisition push alone is an alternative, but October already achieved strong reach growth and it would not resolve the observed gap between first use and durable activity. Broad win-back outreach is another alternative, but the prior frequent-user segment gives a more focused diagnostic starting point. The choice of intervention and any communications require the relevant product/operations owner; this review does not execute them.

Confidence is high in the observed counts and reconciliations, moderate in the interpretation that cohort composition contributes to lower overall depth, and low in any claim about why users lapsed. November should be used to observe retention of the 534 new users and 76 reacquired users. A return to at least 82% overall monthly activity retention is a proposed tracking goal, subject to workforce changes and cohort composition. An eligibility roster, user work schedules and product-exposure or campaign records would help distinguish addressable adoption from staffing and assignment changes. Query logs alone cannot supply that denominator or establish causality.

## 7. Source handling, comparability and limitations

- The seven monthly XLSX extracts are authoritative. Their **154,879 raw records** become **154,515 included query records** after excluding **364 test/smoke/placeholder records**. October contributes 37,798 raw records less 57 excluded records, or 37,741 included records.
- User IDs are trimmed and normalized to lowercase. Exclusions match `test|smoke|demo|admin|service|svc` case-insensitively, plus the exact placeholder `unique_user_identifier`. The observed excluded IDs in these sources are test, smoke and placeholder identifiers.
- Every row has a valid event timestamp and nonempty user ID. Every monthly record falls inside its named month after Pacific conversion. There are no exact duplicate query records across the monthly files.
- September and October each have two repeated user/timestamp pairs containing different question, answer and response-time values. These distinct query records are retained. Timestamp/user alone is therefore not a safe query deduplication key.
- The all-time workbook ends August 24 Pacific and adds zero distinct user/timestamp keys beyond the monthly files. It is not appended. The adjacent August/September CSVs and synthetic datasets are not combined with the authoritative monthly extracts.
- Historical baselines are recalculated consistently. This corrects September MAU from **1,336 in the earlier slide to 1,335**, August from **1,292 to 1,290**, and July from **1,107 to 1,106**. September's reacquired cohort remains **48**. Prior slide WAU figures also used less explicit window conventions, so this review uses one declared rolling-window definition across all months. The old slides are preserved.
- October contains activity through **October 31 at 9:22 p.m. Pacific** after filtering. The supplied workbook is treated as the user's completed monthly capture. The records cannot independently prove that every upstream event was captured.
- These are descriptive statistics of recorded activity. No significance claim is made from adjacent months, overlapping rolling windows, or small reacquisition samples. No workforce roster, role changes, shift exposure, campaign attribution or verified signup dates were supplied.
- The PowerPoint contains one slide following the September v4 visual style, with the October headline metrics, cohort movement and July–October activity charts. All chart values and definitions agree with this summary. File structure, chart data, fonts and layout were checked, and the visual preview was inspected. Native PowerPoint application behavior was not tested.
