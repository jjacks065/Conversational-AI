## Executive slide 1 — Compass will help BSC resolve routine requests and connect callers to informed CSRs

**Takeaway:** Compass can become BSC's first intent gateway for inbound service, resolving supported requests and preserving context when a CSR takes over.

**Suggested slide copy**

- **Resolve routine requests:** begin with high-volume inbound voice intents suitable for self-service.
- **Support CSRs in Shield Advisor:** surface intent, conversation summary, and relevant BSC documents through Compass CSR Chat.
- **Improve service performance:** expand toward fewer transfers, stronger first-contact resolution, and less repeat contact.

**Suggested visual:** a simple service journey: caller expresses need; Compass recognizes intent; supported self-service or CSR handoff with context. Keep the human handoff visible.

**Presenter note:** the proposed distinction is the connection between the voice entry point and the CSR workflow in Shield Advisor. This is a value hypothesis, not a claim of market uniqueness or proven performance. The 12M+ annual call figure may appear as a qualified context note after BSC verifies it.

### Detailed approach for speaker notes

**Stage 0 — December 2026 prototype review**

- Walk BSC stakeholders through representative caller and CSR scenarios.
- Review intent recognition, inbound voice responses, recording/transcription, intent-led document retrieval, and Shield Advisor workflow fit.
- Log gaps with a proposed owner and required decision. Separate prototype issues from production dependencies.
- **Exit evidence:** agreed prototype findings, initial intent shortlist, MVP boundaries, and readiness criteria.
- **Proposed accountable owner:** Compass Product with the BSC contact-center sponsor. Engineering, desktop integration, repository owners, and CSRs contribute.

**Stage 1 — January 2027 preparation and February 2027 MVP**

- Prioritize the first intents by volume, expected resolution value, service risk, and source/system availability.
- Confirm launch population, approved content, necessary authentication/access, recording requirements, escalation handling, and operational support.
- Validate caller and CSR scenarios, including unsupported requests, unclear intent, failed dependencies, and transfer to a person. Define how to restrict or pause automation if service deteriorates.
- Deliver intent and summary in the agreed CSR Chat/Shield Advisor handoff workflow.
- **Exit evidence:** business acceptance of the initial intent set, readiness approval, working measurement, trained support owners, and an accepted fallback process.
- **Proposed accountable owner:** BSC Operations launch owner, supported by Compass Product and Engineering, Shield Advisor owner, Quality, and relevant security/privacy reviewers.

**Stage 2 — Monthly releases, March through June 2027**

- Use Q1 discovery to define monthly scope. Start with high-volume requests that the available data and workflows can reliably resolve.
- Review performance before each release. Fix material quality and handoff problems before adding more volume.
- Track all 20 target intents individually by population, release status, resolution, fallback, and repeat contact.
- **Exit evidence:** the agreed top 20 intents are deployed for their approved populations, measurable deflection is demonstrated, and CSR handoff quality meets approved criteria.
- **Proposed accountable owner:** Compass Product and BSC Operations, with named intent owners and release support.

**Q2 2027 discovery — Phase 2 definition alongside Phase 1 delivery**

- **Routing:** assess current misroutes and transfers, map intents to destinations across all LOBs, define exceptions, and select bounded pilot queues.
- **Digital and callback:** map priority Member, Provider, and Broker journeys; identify context a CSR can use; define scheduling, availability, failed-callback handling, and channel dependencies. Broader digital discovery is proposed.
- **Supervisor experience:** identify repetitive QM, queue-management, and analytical tasks; define outputs that supervisors review and actions requiring human approval. This discovery timing is proposed.
- **Exit evidence:** a pilot charter per selected workstream, with scope, owner, baseline, target, dependencies, support needs, and expansion/stop criteria.
- **Proposed accountable owners:** routing/telephony lead, respective digital channel owners, and supervisor/QM lead. Engineering assesses feasibility and delivery capacity.

**Stage 3 — Q3 2027 pilots, proposed**

- Pilot routing for selected queues/LOBs and compare routing outcomes with the current approach.
- Pilot digital context and enhanced callback on a limited set of journeys before expanding portal coverage.
- Pilot supervisory assistance with human review of quality findings and queue recommendations.
- **Exit evidence:** measured pilot results against the approved baseline and guardrails, acceptable operational reliability, and an expansion recommendation from each accountable owner.
- **Proposed accountable owner:** the relevant BSC workstream owner, with Product, Engineering, and Quality support.

**Stage 4 — Q4 2027 expansion, proposed**

- Expand only capabilities and populations supported by pilot evidence and ready integrations.
- Extend routing coverage progressively; expand portal journeys and callback access by channel readiness.
- Consider predictive engagement where journey signals demonstrate value and required permissions and controls are in place.
- Expand approved supervisory workflows. Keep autonomous queue changes or other material operational actions outside this outline until explicitly scoped and approved.
- **Exit evidence:** agreed service improvements persist as scope expands, operations accepts ongoing ownership, and the next investment decision uses measured outcomes.
- **Proposed accountable owner:** BSC business sponsor and Operations, with accountable channel/workstream owners.

## Supporting collaboration notes

### Strategy, alternatives, and exclusions

**Diagnosis:** the supplied roadmap connects routine call resolution, CSR context, and service operations. Those benefits need separate measures so delivery activity is not mistaken for business improvement.

**Guiding policy:** establish the connected inbound experience first, expand supported intents through June, and use bounded pilots to determine broader investment.

**Coherent actions:** December review; initial February launch; monthly intent expansion; parallel Q2 discovery; Q3 pilots; evidence-based Q4 expansion.

| Alternative | Benefit | Tradeoff and opportunity cost | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Progressive inbound launch with CSR context | Measures value in a bounded scope while preserving the handoff experience. | Enterprise reach arrives gradually. Requires recurring release discipline. | Recommended as the closest fit to the supplied plan. |
| Launch broad routing, portals, and automation together | Could reach more workflows sooner if every dependency is ready. | More integration and change risk, harder attribution of results, and potential distraction from February readiness. | Revisit only with delivery evidence and explicit broader authorization. |
| Focus solely on CSR assistance initially | Reduces dependence on autonomous caller resolution and could deliver agent workflow value earlier. | Delays the front-door and call-deflection objectives. | A contingency if voice readiness cannot support the agreed launch scope. |

**Decision criteria:** time to measurable value, service quality, integration readiness, operating capacity, and ability to attribute outcomes. Staged launches make scope restriction and rollback more manageable, though completed integration effort and training costs are not fully reversible. Evidence that the first intents lack useful volume or cannot resolve requests reliably would challenge the recommendation.

**Explicit exclusions:** outbound voice commitments; a February promise to launch all 20 intents; simultaneous all-LOB/portal deployment; assumed new backend data capabilities; autonomous supervisory decisions; vendor or architecture selection; a validated financial business case. Future requests can scope these separately.

### Outcome and measurement tree

**Primary outcome:** resolve service needs with less customer effort and less avoidable operational work.

| Branch | Baseline and proposed measurement window | Leading indicators | Lagging measures and target status | Guardrails and decision trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Phase 1 self-service | BSC Analytics to establish a representative prelaunch baseline by intent/population. Proposed review weekly during MVP and at 30 days for each release. | Intent recognition quality, supported-scope coverage, task completion, successful fallback. | Successful self-service resolution and net change in live-agent demand. Top-20 coverage is a supplied Phase 1 target. Numeric outcome targets require BSC approval before launch. | Track abandonment, complaints, repeat contact, and answer quality. Pause expansion if approved limits fail. |
| CSR effectiveness | Baseline current information search and handoff performance before the pilot; compare equivalent intents/cohorts after launch. | Intent/summary completeness, document relevance, CSR adoption. | Time to usable information and first-contact resolution. Numeric targets require baseline agreement. | No deterioration in answer correctness or necessary access to a human. Rework handoff if CSRs cannot use its context. |
| Routing and journey continuity | Baseline misroutes, transfers, repeat contact, and satisfaction by selected journey/LOB in Q2. Proposed reviews at 30 and 60 days of each pilot. | Correct destination selection, usable journey context, callback completion. | Lower misroutes/transfers and repeat contact, better first-contact resolution and satisfaction. Quantitative thresholds are a Q2 discovery output. | Evaluate AHT with case mix, resolution, and satisfaction. Restrict routing scope if transfers or failures increase beyond approved limits. |
| Supervisor productivity | Establish manual effort per QM review, queue review, and reporting task before the pilot. Proposed comparison over the first 30–60 days. | Supervisor use, agreement with reviewed findings, actionable insight rate. | Less manual effort with maintained or improved review quality. Numeric targets require supervisor/QM approval. | Track missed issues and false findings. Keep consequential actions subject to approved human review. |

**Measurement definitions to agree:** distinguish containment from successful self-service resolution. For this draft, the latter means a supported request completes without CSR assistance and without a repeat contact for the same issue within an agreed window. Analytics must establish how repeat contact can be linked and how unresolved cases are treated. A seven-day repeat-contact window is an option for discussion, not an approved rule.

**Attribution:** report eligible interactions and populations explicitly. Compare like-for-like intent cohorts and account for seasonality, channel shifts, and changing case mix. Do not multiply an unvalidated deflection rate by all 12M+ annual calls.
