1. Implementation Overview
CSR Chat implementation is a joint program between Stellarus and the health plan. The work combines benefit-data onboarding, workflow and answer design, identity integration, customer validation, representative readiness, and controlled production rollout.
Implementation outcome
A validated CSR Chat experience that uses the health plan's approved data, authenticates the intended users, fits the selected representative workflow, meets agreed quality and performance criteria, and transitions to supported production operations.

1.1 Delivery principles
Start with a bounded representative group, plan population, and benefit scope.
Use customer-approved source data and customer-supplied request context.
Define answer correctness and launch criteria before configuration begins.
Validate with the health plan's benefit subject matter experts and business owner.
Move through staging, pilot, and production using explicit entry and exit gates.

1.3 Success criteria
Implementation success criteria are agreed during discovery and measured before pilot and production. They typically cover:
Source-data completeness, effective dating, and refresh reliability.
Answer accuracy and required-disclosure compliance against an agreed question set.
Response-time and availability expectations for the selected delivery model.
Identity, access, and security approval.
Representative readiness, training completion, and support procedures.
Pilot adoption, feedback, and agreed production-exit criteria.

3. Implementation Timeline
Implementation follows six gated phases. The calendar is baselined after discovery because source-data readiness, security review, integration depth, customer review cycles, and subject matter expert availability determine the critical path.
Timeline planning basis
Use the phase sequence below as the baseline plan. Stellarus and the health plan assign dates, dependencies, and exit criteria during Phase 1. No fixed duration should be treated as a commitment before discovery is complete.

5.3 Plan context and refresh
The calling application supplies the plan context with each question. CSR Chat does not search for a member or determine which plan applies. During discovery, the teams define the identifiers that make up a plan reference, including any effective date required to select the applicable version.
Reconcile source counts and identifiers before configuration begins.
Validate that the same plan context is used consistently across environments.
Test changed and future-dated plans before production refreshes.
Define how failed, partial, or late refreshes are detected and resolved.

6.2 Authentication
All CSR Chat access is routed through the Stellarus AI Gateway; underlying services are not directly exposed.
Representatives authenticate through the health plan identity provider using OAuth 2.0 for the hosted experience.
System-to-system integrations use customer-specific credentials that are issued and rotated through the agreed operating process.
Roles, groups, provisioning, deprovisioning, and environment access are confirmed during design.
Detailed authorization controls, trust boundaries, and security evidence are maintained in the Technical Architecture & Security Overview.

8.2 Pilot
Enable a limited, trained representative population.
Monitor answer quality, response time, errors, adoption, and feedback.
Review issues jointly and route them to data, configuration, integration, training, or product owners.
Apply approved adjustments in staging before production release.
Exit when the business owner confirms that agreed pilot criteria are met.
8.3 Production rollout
Expand access according to the approved representative rollout plan.
Confirm production identity groups, support paths, monitoring, and escalation.
Transition the integrated plan, decisions, known limitations, and open risks to steady-state owners.
Establish the recurring review cadence for performance, quality, feedback, data refreshes, and product changes.

9.1 Change control
Changes to plan scope, source data, identifiers, answer behavior, delivery model, security requirements, or launch criteria are documented with their impact on timeline, validation, and responsibilities. Material changes require joint approval before they are added to the implementation baseline.
